01

Research & lit review

Overview — what this whole thing is

We are teaching a robotic arm (NERO) to manipulate objects by imitating human demonstrations instead of being hand-programmed. The pipeline looks like this:

  1. CollectTeleoperated demonstrations — camera frames plus the arm's joint positions, recorded as episodes.
  2. Audit & cleanAudit the dataset, purge bad episodes, smooth noisy trajectories.
  3. TrainTrain an imitation-learning policy that maps camera images to the arm's next actions.
  4. EvaluateRun the policy on the real arm, measure success rate, feed failures back into data collection.

The open question this log tracks: which training policy should NERO use? Our shortlist from the literature: ACT, Diffusion Policy, and NVIDIA's GR00T.

Training policies — ACT vs Diffusion vs GR00T

Lightweight baseline

ACT

Action Chunking with Transformers — Zhao, Kumar, Levine, Finn (2023), the ALOHA low-cost bimanual system.

  • Predicts a chunk of the next k joint actions at once instead of one step at a time, which fights the compounding-error problem of imitation learning.
  • Transformer over ResNet-18 camera features + joint positions, with a latent style variable (CVAE).
  • ~80M parameters; trains in a few hours on a single GPU; high success rates from ~50 demonstrations.
  • On ALOHA: 80–90% success on 6 fine tasks (e.g. slotting a battery, opening a cup) from about 10 minutes of demos.
  • First-class support in Hugging Face LeRobot — the recommended starting point.

Stronger modelling

Diffusion Policy

Chi, Xu, Feng et al. (2023) — the visuomotor policy as a conditional denoising diffusion process.

  • Instead of outputting one action, it refines noise into an action sequence over several denoising steps, conditioned on camera images.
  • Handles multimodal demonstrations (several valid ways to do a task) that break plain regression policies.
  • Receding-horizon control: re-plans every step while keeping the motion temporally consistent.
  • Average improvement of 46.9% over prior state of the art across 12 tasks in 4 benchmarks.
  • Costs more compute per inference step than ACT (iterative denoising).

Foundation model

GR00T

NVIDIA Isaac GR00T N1 (2025) — an open foundation model for generalist humanoid robots.

  • Dual-system design: a vision-language model (System 2) reasons about the scene and instruction; a diffusion transformer (System 1) turns the plan into continuous actions.
  • Cross-embodiment: one set of weights adapts to different robots through post-training on your own demos.
  • Trained on a large humanoid dataset plus synthetic and internet-video data; part of the dataset is open on Hugging Face.
  • NVIDIA reports GR00T N1 2B averaging 76.8% vs Diffusion Policy's 46.4% on its benchmark suite (vendor-reported).
  • Much heavier to run and fine-tune than ACT — likely overkill for a first arm.

02

What we learnt

Data quality beats model cleverness

Every method we read is only as good as its demonstrations. Imitation-learning errors compound over time, so auditing and cleaning the dataset is not a chore around the project — it is the project.

Chunking is the cheap fix for drift

ACT's core trick — predicting a short sequence of actions at once — directly attacks compounding error, and it trains in hours on one GPU. Good enough to be our baseline.

Diffusion handles "there's more than one right move"

When demos contain several valid strategies, a plain regression policy averages them into a bad one. Diffusion Policy models the whole distribution instead, at the price of slower inference.

Foundation models are a later problem

GR00T shows where the field is heading — one generalist model, post-trained per robot — but its compute cost makes it a poor first step for a student arm. Revisit once a baseline works.

Plain RGB is a legitimate starting point

ALOHA reaches 80–90% success using commodity webcams with no depth sensor. Depth adds geometric information but also hardware, sync and calibration overhead — a trade-off to test, not assume.

Tooling exists — don't build it

Hugging Face LeRobot ships dataset formats, training scripts and ACT out of the box. Our pipeline should build on it rather than reinvent it.

03

Ideation & development

Step 1

Dataset auditing

Watch every recorded episode, flag failures and out-of-distribution moments, and keep a manifest of what each episode contains. Decide acceptance criteria before collecting more.

Step 2

Trajectory cleaning & smoothing

Remove jitter and teleoperation noise from joint trajectories (e.g. low-pass filtering), fix timestamps and dropped frames so the policy learns smooth motion.

Step 3

Cleaning & purging data

Delete corrupted or failed episodes, deduplicate near-identical segments, and rebalance the dataset so the policy doesn't overfit one behaviour.

Step 4

Camera: RGB vs RGB‑D

Benchmark both. Start RGB-only (matching ACT/ALOHA); add depth if tasks need geometry that colour alone can't give, weighing the extra calibration and sync work.
